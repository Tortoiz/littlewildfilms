The first version of the forest looked beautiful, but it felt empty.

It had the right trees, the right light, and the right atmosphere. But it did not feel like a place that had existed before the camera arrived.

## The problem with beautiful images

When an image is too perfect, there is nowhere for the viewer’s imagination to enter.

For *The Lantern in the Orchard*, we began introducing small imperfections: paths that disappeared, trees that leaned toward the light, and details that felt almost remembered rather than clearly designed.

## Leaving room for the viewer

The most important part of the forest is not what we show. It is what the frame leaves unresolved.

A distant light can be a home, a memory, or just a reflection on wet grass. The film does not need to decide immediately. It only needs to make the question feel worth carrying into the next shot.

> A world becomes believable when it has a life outside the frame.

This is the kind of quiet uncertainty we are trying to protect as the film grows.
